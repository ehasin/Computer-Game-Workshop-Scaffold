# Run of show — 4 to 5 hours

The one-page version. Full detail, prompts and troubleshooting are in
[FACILITATOR_HANDBOOK.md](FACILITATOR_HANDBOOK.md).

Timings are guides; compress the middle blocks if the group is fast. **Don't
compress the deploy or the wrap-up** — the ending is what they remember.

| # | Block | Time | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Circle: context and intro | 15 min | Circle, no screens |
| 1 | Q&A: game, AI, UX, agile, the three roles | 20 min | Circle, no screens |
| 2 | Idea brainstorm + feasibility + naming + domain | 30 min | Big screen, ChatGPT |
| 3 | 🍕 Pizza | 30 min | Table, no laptops |
| 4 | Two groups in parallel: art desk / build desk | 60–75 min | Zones A + B |
| 5 | Integrate the art: scan → render → wire in → test | 45 min | Everyone together |
| 6 | Polish, deploy, activate the domain, play on phones | 30 min | Big screen + test bench |
| 7 | Summary circle: learned / favourite role / next time | 15 min | Circle, screens off |

## The lessons hiding in each block

- **Block 2 — lean prototyping and agile scoping.** Every idea gets a
  red/amber/green cost estimate before anyone writes code. The *rejections*
  teach more than the acceptances.
- **Block 4 (build) — the walking skeleton.** One thin playable slice with
  grey boxes: win, lose, score, one mechanic. Not every feature.
- **Block 4 (iteration) — prompt engineering.** Each addition is a
  one-sentence user story, implemented, playtested immediately, kept or cut.
  Better-specified prompts produce better first drafts, visibly.
- **Block 5 — UX.** Does the custom art make the game easier or harder to
  read at a glance? Readability versus personalisation is a real trade-off,
  and the kids make the call.
- **Block 6 — shipping is a skill.** Deploying, a real domain, a link that
  works on someone else's phone.
- **Block 7 — retrospective.** What did *we* decide, what did the *AI* do
  for us, and where is the line between the two.

## The one rule for the whole day

**The game is playable at every moment.** Every change is a small, shippable
step. If something breaks, revert first and debug second — a working game is
the baseline you never give up.
